## Foreword

This is a fictional story—the characters and plot are all made up. So why did I still put it on my blog? In my view, a story is also a kind of thinking; only the content of that thinking isn't knowledge, but rather reflections on what I've seen in life, what I feel inside, and so on. So it fits my idea of a "blog," and that's why I put it up.


## Main Text

Xidan Joy City, a mall that smells wonderful, filled with the scents of cedar, sandalwood, fig, green tea, white tea, and vetiver. One of the few interesting malls in Beijing.

I'd originally come to browse with my roommate, but not long after we walked in, his lab called and urged him to go back to work. I had no choice but to go to Pop Mart alone on his behalf and buy the Valorant collaboration items and a bottle of perfume. By the time I wandered over to the dining area with the blind-box bag in hand, it was already completely dark outside.

I sat at the entrance of a small hot pot place waiting for my number, head down, scrolling on my phone.

Suddenly, a very ordinary pair of AJs stopped in the corner of my vision.

I instinctively looked up and saw her.

She was someone from my high school days whom even I couldn't say for sure whether I "liked" or not. If you say I didn't like her—well, it's been so long since graduation, and I still think of her from time to time, like today. But if you say I did like her, I don't think it really counts. Throughout all of high school, the words we exchanged added up to no more than five times—it was purely me having a one-sided crush, and only because she was pretty. Back then I was like a clueless kid, looking dopey and unable to hold a conversation. After we graduated from high school, we never contacted each other again.

Unexpectedly, she was the one who recognized me first, standing in front of me with a smile and saying hello.

After a bit of small talk, and seeing that my number was almost up, something stirred in my heart, and I spoke up:

"Emmmmm, I want to say something. I think it's a little polite and a little impolite, and I don't know if I should say it."

"What is it?"

"Do you want to eat together?"

She paused for a moment, her eyes and brows curving into a smile: "Can I?"

"Of course you can."

Just then my number was called, and we walked in together.

The small hot pots were soon brought over, the clear broth bubbling away, steam slowly rising between the two of us, putting a layer of separation between us and the noise of the mall around us. Halfway through the meal, I casually asked her, "So why are you here alone?"

"I went to a concert at Wukesong last night," she said, picking up a piece of vegetable, her tone light. "I'd originally made plans to meet a friend here tonight, but she had something come up at the last minute and couldn't make it. My high-speed train back to school is early tomorrow morning, so I took Line 1 over here to wander around."

In the swirling steam, we chatted on and off about trivial things at our respective schools, and it didn't feel so awkward after all. Partway through, I excused myself to go get dipping sauce and took the opportunity to pay the bill at the front desk. When we got up to leave after eating, she instinctively pulled out her phone: "Don't I need to pay?"

"No need. The front desk said you're too pretty, so they let you eat for free."

She froze for a second, then couldn't help laughing out loud.

After dinner it was still early, and she had no other plans, so she came along with me to pick out perfume.

We visited four stores: Perfume Box, Burberry, Chloé, and a fragrance shop around the corner whose name I can't remember. In each one, the warm white spotlights made rows of glass bottles gleam translucently. We stood at the counter, took the test strips, gently waved them in the air a couple of times, waited for the initial alcohol scent to fade, then brought them close to our noses to smell, and naturally handed them to each other.

The top notes were crisp and cool, the middle notes slowly warming; some were too boldly sweet, others as deep as wood after rain. After smelling for a while, our noses gradually went a bit numb—no matter which strip we brought close, they all seemed to smell the same. The shop assistant smiled and handed over a small jar of coffee beans; we each leaned in and took a deep breath, and the slightly bitter roasted aroma gave our sense of smell a jolt—it really did clear up instantly. After four stores, we each had a small handful of test strips bearing different fragrance notes, and even our fingertips and cuffs were tinged with a blended, gentle scent.

After we'd been through them all, I did a quick calculation of my budget and still led her back to Burberry, where I picked out the one that cost five hundred yuan a bottle.

Standing at the counter, I said to the sales assistant: "Miss, I'll take two bottles. One for myself, and one to give to my friend here."

She was clearly taken aback and quickly waved her hands: "Huh? I don't use it."

"It's for you, it's fine. I'll tell you why in a bit."

The sales assistant behind the counter immediately gave a knowing look, her eyes quietly darting between the two of us, an undisguisable grin of gossip on her face, as she deftly packed the two bottles of perfume into a bag for us. Before handing it over, she picked up the tester on the counter and, with a smile, sprayed each of us once on the spot.

A fine mist settled gently on our collars and wrists, a clear freshness wrapped in a restrained warmth, quickly and quietly subduing the jumbled scents we'd picked up along the way.

When we pushed open the glass door of Joy City and stepped out, the warm light behind us was shut inside the door all at once.

Perhaps I will always remember this day. Beijing's autumn wind has always been like a supreme sovereign—clearly just sitting there, doing nothing, yet carrying an inherent majesty that makes people bow their heads and crawl forward. But for some reason, walking out into this wind tonight, I didn't find it cold at all.

The tall ginkgo tree by the plaza was lit from below by ground lamps, its canopy of gold glaring bright against the deep blue night sky, with scattered pedestrians standing beneath it taking photos. A gust of wind swept down from the treetops, lifting a few strands of her hair and brushing them lightly across her cheek. She raised a hand to tuck the loose strands behind her ear, turned her head to look at me, and the wind carried her voice away, thin and soft.

"So can you tell me now? Why did you suddenly give me perfume?"

I didn't answer right away, looking down at a few ginkgo leaves spinning at my feet, chased by the wind.

"Actually... it's nothing." After a couple of steps, I felt that brushing it off like that wasn't quite right, so I went on, "I just felt like, we barely talked back in high school, and now we're not even in the same city."

Wind poured in through my collar, and I softened my voice: "Running into you today was already quite a coincidence. I feel like... this is probably the last time we'll see each other."

After I said it, I wasn't sure how she'd react. As I'd always known, I really knew almost nothing about her.

She stopped under the ginkgo tree, looked at me for two seconds, and then suddenly gave a soft laugh, the warm lights of the plaza reflected in her eyes.

"You seem... different from how you were in high school," she said.

Before I could respond, she had already raised her phone.

"Like you said, this might be the last time we see each other. Let's take a photo together."

The golden ginkgo leaves rustled overhead, the lights of Joy City glowed behind us, and the shutter clicked softly.

After the photo, she looked down and opened WeChat, meaning to send it to me. Her finger scrolled down several screens, and after searching for quite a while without finding my profile picture, she couldn't help but laugh at herself before finally remembering to tap the search bar at the top. She typed in my name, and the chat that popped up was almost blank—the last message still dated 2024.

After she sent the photo, we walked slowly along the sidewalk to the entrance of Xidan subway station and waved goodbye with a smile at the top of the stairs. I stood in the wind and watched her carry that Burberry paper bag down the steps, quickly merging into the crowd of late-night commuters.

It was already late when I got back to the dorm. I took off my jacket and hung it on the bed, and my nose suddenly brushed against a faint scent lingering on the collar—the spray the sales assistant had given it on the spot before I left the counter, now reduced to a faint base note mixed with the chill of the night wind.

My phone buzzed on the desk. The screen lit up with a new message from her, right below tonight's photo and that old record from 2024:

"I had a great time today. Thank you for the perfume."

I know, really, that time has never played fair. In a few years, tonight's ginkgo, the steam from the hot pot, even the way she smiled as she raised her phone—all of it will probably sink, bit by bit, to the very bottom of memory, just like that chat record from 2024, and I may not even dig out the photo again. I just hope the scent of this five-hundred-yuan bottle lingers a little longer—long enough that by the time I can no longer pick her out in a crowd, the autumn wind will still be willing to remember this night for me.
