Xidan Joy City, a mall that smells wonderful, filled with the scents of cedar, sandalwood, fig, green tea, white tea, and vetiver. One of the few interesting malls in Beijing.

I had originally come here to hang out with my roommate, but not long after we walked in, his lab called and urged him to go back to work. I had no choice but to go to Pop Mart alone on his behalf to buy the Valorant collaboration items and a bottle of perfume. By the time I wandered over to the dining area with the blind box bag in hand, it was already completely dark outside.

I sat at the entrance of a small hot pot place waiting for my number to be called, head down, scrolling on my phone.

Suddenly, a very ordinary pair of AJs stopped in the corner of my vision.

I instinctively looked up and saw her.

She was someone from my high school days whom even I myself couldn't say for sure whether I "liked" or not. If you say I didn't like her, well, it's been so long since graduation and I still think of her from time to time, like today; but if you say I did like her, I don't think it really counts. Throughout all of high school, the total number of words we exchanged didn't exceed five—it was purely me having a one-sided crush on her, and only because she was pretty. Back then I was like a clueless kid, looking dopey and unable to hold a conversation, and after high school graduation, we never contacted each other again.

Unexpectedly, she was the one who recognized me first, standing in front of me with a smile and saying hello.

After a couple of polite pleasantries, seeing that my number was almost up, something stirred in my heart, and I spoke up:

"Emmmmm, I want to say something. I think it's a bit polite and a bit impolite, and I don't know if I should say it."

"What is it?"

"Do you want to eat together?"

She paused for a moment, her eyes and brows curving into a smile: "Can I?"

"Of course you can."

Just then my number was called, and we walked in together.

The small hot pots were soon brought over, the clear broth bubbling away, steam slowly rising between the two of us, creating a layer of separation from the noise of the mall around us. Halfway through the meal, I casually asked her, "So why are you here alone?"

"I went to a concert at Wukesong last night," she said, picking up a piece of vegetable, her tone light. "I had originally made plans with a friend who's here, but she had something come up at the last minute and couldn't make it. I have an early high-speed train back to school tomorrow morning, so I took Line 1 over here to wander around."

Amid the swirling steam, we chatted on and off about trivial things at our respective schools, and it didn't feel that awkward after all. Partway through, I excused myself to go get dipping sauce and took the opportunity to pay the bill at the front desk. When we got up to leave after eating, she instinctively pulled out her phone: "Don't I need to pay?"

"No need. The front desk said you're too pretty, so they let you eat for free."

She froze for a second, then couldn't help but burst out laughing.

After dinner it was still early, and she didn't have any other plans, so she came along with me to pick out perfume.

We visited four stores: Perfume Box, Burberry, Chloé, and a fragrance shop at the corner whose name I can't remember. In each one, the warm white spotlights made rows of glass bottles gleam translucently. We stood at the counter, took the test strips, gently waved them in the air a couple of times, waited for the initial alcohol scent to dissipate, then brought them close to our noses to smell, and naturally handed them to each other.

The top notes were crisp and cool, the middle notes gradually warming; some were too assertively sweet, others deep like wood after rain. After a while, our noses gradually went a bit numb—no matter which strip we brought close, they all seemed to smell the same. The shop assistant smiled and handed over a small jar of coffee beans; we each leaned in and took a deep breath, and the slightly bitter roasted aroma gave our sense of smell a jolt—it really did clear up instantly. After visiting all four stores, we each had accumulated a small handful of test strips bearing different fragrance notes, and even our fingertips and cuffs had taken on a blended, gentle scent.

After we'd finished browsing everywhere, I did a quick mental calculation of my budget and still led her back to Burberry, where I picked out the one that cost five hundred yuan a bottle.

Standing at the counter, I said to the sales assistant: "Miss, I'll take two bottles. One for myself, and one to give to my friend here."

She was clearly taken aback and quickly waved her hands: "Huh? I don't use perfume."

"It's for you, it's fine. I'll tell you why in a bit."

The sales assistant behind the counter immediately put on a knowing expression, her eyes quietly darting between the two of us, an irrepressible grin of gossip on her face, as she deftly packed the two bottles of perfume into a bag. Before handing it over, she picked up the tester on the table and, with a smile, gave each of us a spritz on the spot.

Fine mist settled gently on our collars and wrists, a clear freshness wrapped in a restrained warmth, quickly and quietly suppressing the jumbled scents we'd picked up along the way.

When we pushed open the glass door of Joy City and stepped out, the warm light behind us was shut inside the door all at once.

Perhaps I will always remember this day. Beijing's autumn wind has always been like a supreme monarch—clearly just sitting there, doing nothing, yet possessing an inherent majesty that makes people bow their heads and crawl forward. But for some reason, walking out into this wind tonight, I didn't find it cold at all.

The tall ginkgo tree by the plaza was lit from below by ground lights, its canopy of golden leaves dazzling against the deep blue night sky, with pedestrians standing in small groups beneath it taking photos. A gust of wind swept down from the ginkgo treetops, lifting a few strands of her hair and brushing them gently across her cheek. She raised her hand to tuck the stray hair behind her ear, turned her head to look at me, and the wind carried her voice away, soft and light.

"Now can you tell me? Why did you suddenly give me perfume?"

I didn't answer right away, looking down at a few ginkgo leaves swirling at my feet, carried by the wind.

"Actually... it's nothing much." After a couple of steps, I felt that brushing it off like that wasn't great, so I continued, "I just felt that we barely talked in high school, and now we're not even in the same city."

The wind poured into my collar, and I softened my voice a little: "It's already quite a coincidence that we ran into each other today. I feel like... this is probably the last time we'll meet."

After saying that, I didn't dare to guess how she would react. As I had always known, I actually knew almost nothing about her.

She stopped under the ginkgo tree, looked at me for two seconds, and suddenly gave a soft laugh, the warm lights of the plaza reflected in her eyes.

"You seem a little different from how you were in high school," she said.

Before I could respond, she had already raised her phone.

"Like you said, this might be the last time we meet, so let's take a photo together."

The golden ginkgo leaves rustled overhead, the lights of Joy City glowed behind us, and the shutter clicked softly.

After the photo, she lowered her head and opened WeChat, intending to send it to me. Her fingers swiped down several screens, and after searching for a long while without finding my profile picture, she couldn't help but laugh at herself before finally remembering to tap the search box at the top. She entered my name, and the chat that popped up was almost blank, with the last message still dated 2024.

After the photo came through, we walked slowly along the sidewalk to the entrance of Xidan subway station and waved goodbye with a smile at the top of the stairs. I stood in the wind and watched her carry that Burberry paper bag down the steps, quickly merging into the crowd of late-night commuters.

It was already very late when I got back to the dorm. I took off my jacket and hung it on the bed, and my nose suddenly brushed against a faint scent lingering on the collar—it was from the spray the sales assistant had given me right before I left the counter, now only a faint base note remained, mixed with a bit of the night wind's chill.

My phone buzzed on the desk. The screen lit up with a new message from her, right below tonight's photo and that old record from 2024:

"I had a great time today, thank you for the perfume."

Actually, I know that time has never played fair. In a few years, tonight's ginkgo, the steam from the hot pot, and even the way she smiled as she raised her phone will probably, like that 2024 chat record, slowly sink to the very bottom of memory, and I may not even bother to look at the photo again. I just hope the scent I bought for five hundred yuan will last a little longer—long enough that one day, when I can no longer recognize her in a crowd, the autumn wind will still be willing to remember this night for me.
